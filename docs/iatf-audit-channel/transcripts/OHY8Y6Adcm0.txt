In this video, the auditor is auditing the production process of glass molding. A good practice is to ask the operator to give an overview of the process before getting into detailed questions. At this stage, the supervisor tries to take over and explain but correctly the auditor politely states that he wants the operator to explain. Another good practice before asking detailed question is to observe the process. in this case the molding and then question the assembly operator to help ensure the correct insertion of the washers. The operator explains they have an assembly aid to help. While this cannot be classified as errorproofing as it is not error prevention, it is certainly a method of reducing the risk of incorrect washer insertion. The auditor asks the question about based upon the operator experience what can go wrong with the process. Once answered, this would give a good opportunity to check some of the potential failure modes in the PFMEA and verify the defined controls are in place linked to the control plan. The auditor observes the operator marking the product with her initials after the visual inspection, which the operator explains is to help with the traceability in the event of internal or external issues downstream either internally or at the customer. This relates to IETF requirement 8.5.2. 2.1 identification and traceability supplemental which includes a statement. The organization shall conduct analysis of internal customer and regulatory traceability requirements for all automotive products including development and documenting traceability plans based on the levels of risk of failure severity for employees, customers and consumers. There is an opportunity for the auditor to follow audit trails related to this. For example, to check the product marking requirements, for example, the date marking on the product from the molding tool and other related traceability records. The auditor then questions the lighting in the area as the product in question will be seen on the exterior of a vehicle. IETF requirement 8.6.3 appearance items is likely to be relevant. The requirement states for organizations manufacturing parts designated by the customer as appearance items, the organization shall provide the following. Maintenance and control of appearance masters and evaluation equipment. D. verification that personnel making appearance evaluations are competent and qualified to do so. The auditor states he will follow up on the control of lighting with maintenance later in the audit. Finally, the auditor asks the operator to show how they have access to the relevant process documentation. The operator shows an instruction posted in the area and then takes the auditor to a computer station close to the process to show some of the electronic documentation. This relates to IETF requirement 8.5.1.2 Two, standardize work operator instructions and visual standards. This states the organization ensure that the standardized work documents are a communicated to and understood by the employees who are responsible performing the work. B they be legible. C presented in a language understood by the personnel responsible to follow them. D accessible for use at the designated work areas. The standardized work document shall also include rules for operator safety. Note in this requirement it does not require documents to be in hard copy format. The key thing is for the auditor to verify the audit in this case the operator is familiar with where to locate the relevant documentation and that they are complying with the relevant requirements.
