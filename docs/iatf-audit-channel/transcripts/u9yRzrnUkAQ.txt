Firstly, it's good practice that before going to the warehouse to audit a process, the auditor has reviewed the relevant KPI data including on-time delivery and right first time. In the warehouse, the auditee introduces himself and explains that a consultant is present. According to IATF rules, if this were a third-party auditor, the auditor should have asked the consultant to leave the audit. The auditee explains the receiving process and how the supplier barcode is used to manage it, traceability, inspection and test status, and how it could not be used if the chemical composition had not been verified. This links to IATF 16949 requirement 8.5.2 traceability, 8.6.4 verification and acceptance, and 10.2.4 error proofing. Once the auditor has gained an overview of the process, they should then test the process by getting the auditee to scan some coils and then review the traceability, inspection, and test status. To test the error proofing, the auditor could have asked the auditee to locate a coil that had not been tested, move it to the cutting process, and see if it could be used.
