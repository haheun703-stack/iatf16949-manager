A good practice is to observe the setup and completion of the test. It was clear that the auditor did not understand the testing process, but focused on the equipment calibration. Viewing the certificate for the masters and the master test block. The auditor seemed to be trying to impose that all equipment, including the master test blocks, had to be calibrated annually, which is incorrect. The frequency should be determined by the client based on risk. The auditor then incorrectly stated the gauge R&R must be undertaken for all equipment specified on the control plan, which is wrong from two perspectives. 7.1.5.1.1 states that MSA must be undertaken for each type of measurement system specified on the control plan. There is no requirement for gauge R&R, and as the client explained, more suitable studies could be bias, linearity, and stability using the master test blocks. In summary, this was not an effective audit, and the auditor lacked the technical competency to understand an effective audit of this technical laboratory testing process.
